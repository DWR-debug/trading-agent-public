# Deep Public Information Frontier — 2026-10-03

This discovery layer extends the research frontier from conventional market/macro datasets into public information generated by agencies, regulators, infrastructure operators and public administrative systems.

## Research rule

A channel is interesting only when it contains information that is economically distinct from OHLCV and has a reconstructable information boundary. The first stage is source feasibility plus synthetic future-mutation testing. A source that cannot be reconstructed as-of a decision point remains a negative result, not a reason to approximate it.

## Q152 — House transaction disclosure × exposed-industry state

Public House financial-disclosure reports expose periodic transaction reports. The candidate is not a political forecast; it is a mechanically defined information-timing channel. First feasibility must establish public posting time, transaction date semantics and a frozen issuer/industry mapping.

## Q153 — Lobbying-intensity transition × issuer/industry exposure

LDA.gov provides public downloadable registration and report records through a REST API, including anonymous access subject to stricter throttling. The research signal would be a fixed transition in disclosed lobbying activity for a pre-registered issuer/industry map, never a hand-scored issue sentiment state.

## Q154 — Drug shortage/manufacturing shock × pharma exposure

openFDA publishes public drug datasets including recalls, Drugs@FDA and drug shortages. A candidate state can encode a newly public shortage/recall/manufacturing event for firms mapped to the affected product or manufacturer. The first gate must separate occurrence, database update and public observation times.

## Q155 — Medical-device recall/enforcement shock × manufacturer exposure

openFDA exposes medical-device recalls, enforcement reports, adverse events and registrations/listings. This creates an operational supply/regulatory information channel that is distinct from price and generic news.

## Q156 — Cyber exploitation shock × issuer technology exposure

CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog is an authoritative public list of vulnerabilities exploited in the wild and is distributed in CSV and JSON. NVD adds publication-date filters. A fixed issuer/product map could translate newly published/exploited vulnerabilities into an event-state without using future incident outcomes.

## Q157 — SEC FOIA topic heat × issuer information-demand state

SEC FOIA logs are public CSVs covering requests from January 2006 through August 2026. They contain request metadata and provide a potential state variable for unusually concentrated information demand. The first gate must ensure that request receipt, description and closure fields are not misinterpreted as market information known earlier.

## Q158 — Federal Register rule-change exposure × industry

GovInfo exposes machine-readable Federal Register and related congressional/legal collections through its bulk repository. This can support a fixed event taxonomy for newly published rules/orders, with a next-session boundary unless exact dissemination timing can be established.

## Q159 — Disaster shock × geographic operating exposure

FEMA disaster declarations and NOAA Storm Events can be combined with a frozen geographic exposure map. The hypothesis is not that disasters predict returns mechanically, but that exogenous operational disruption may create heterogeneous information states for firms with concentrated exposure.

## Q160 — Agricultural release shock × food/input-chain exposure

USDA NASS Quick Stats is a broad public agricultural database and major reports also have JSON files at release time. A fixed release-state compiler can combine crop/livestock observations with a pre-frozen issuer/commodity exposure map.

## Q161 — Freight-flow composition shock × industrial exposure

BTS Freight Analysis Framework provides public freight-flow structure across modes and commodities. A future candidate can use changes in composition or route pressure as a slow-moving supply-chain state, with historical dataset vintages explicitly frozen.

## Q162 — Power-system stress × electricity-intensive issuer state

EIA provides public electricity collections and bulk downloads. A candidate can use an operational grid/load state interacting with a fixed issuer energy-intensity classification. Continuous updates make the information boundary the key research problem.

## Q163 — Environmental enforcement shock × facility-exposed issuer

EPA Envirofacts aggregates multiple public environmental systems and provides REST access, joins, geography and machine-readable output. A fixed environmental-action taxonomy plus facility-to-issuer mapping creates an unusual regulatory/operational channel.

## Q164 — Federal award concentration × contractor ecosystem

USAspending has public endpoints that do not require authorization and exposes award-search and reporting-publication information. The research design is a fixed graph of issuer/contractor exposure and newly published award transitions, with amendments separated from initial dissemination.

## Q165 — Competition-enforcement shock × industry network

FTC provides historical merger and nonmerger enforcement datasets; DOJ Antitrust provides public releases. A fixed event taxonomy and peer graph can test whether public competition-enforcement shocks alter information states across exposed firms.

## Cross-channel combinations

The most interesting second-stage combinations are deliberately not ranked by expected returns. They are combinations of different clocks:
- operational shock + public regulatory disclosure;
- supply-chain flow + government award;
- cyber exploitation + issuer disclosure;
- environmental event + disaster state;
- agricultural release + weather state;
- lobbying/disclosure intensity + regulatory event.

Each combination must first pass source and PIT gates independently. No composite can inherit feasibility merely because its component sources are individually available.

## Scientific boundary

All candidates remain discovery-only. No candidate authorizes performance, holdout selection, parameter/threshold/horizon search, asset selection, ranking, promotion or live execution.
